## Overview

The Deferred Payment Agreement (DPA) purportedly in place for Bridget Walker is defective at virtually every level — in its formation, its execution, its content, and its ongoing application. The issues identified below span misrepresentation, economic duress, document integrity, contractual ambiguity, unlawful charging, and the absence of any valid agreement covering the placement where Bridget has resided since April 2023.

An independent 2026 guide to DPAs confirms the core principle at stake: where a mandatory property disregard applies, **the property is taken out of the financial assessment entirely — meaning no DPA is needed and no charge can be placed**. The council should never have offered a DPA at all.

## 1. Formation Failures

### 1.1 Five Months of Charges Without a Signed Agreement

The DPA states care charges commenced on **20 December 2022**. The agreement was not signed until **19 May 2023** — a gap of five months. The council was operating as if a DPA existed throughout this period with no contractual authority whatsoever. Every charge accumulated before 19 May 2023 was raised without any signed legal basis.

### 1.2 "Do Not Date" — Date Inserted Unilaterally

The council sent two copies of the DPA and CH1 both marked **"DO NOT DATE"** — confirmed by the physical document retained by Mark Walker showing this instruction handwritten on both the DPA date field and the CH1 date field. The council then unilaterally inserted the date of 19 May 2023 after receiving the signed undated copies back, without the knowledge or consent of the signatories.

**Key email evidence (3 May 2023):** Lee Forder confirmed the council had received one signed copy and stated: *"once manager has signed them both one will be returned to you."* The manager's signature section was blank when the contracts were returned. The council therefore completed their own execution and inserted the date after receipt — without informing the attorneys of the date they had chosen.

### 1.3 Signed Under Economic Duress

The DPA was signed under economic duress. The council had been invoicing at £800/week without a signed agreement, sending formal reminders threatening escalation, and demanding £615 be paid before the DPA could even be applied. Mark Walker had also already paid Westcliff Lodge invoices directly and was simultaneously facing mounting council debt demands. No reasonable alternative to signing existed. No independent advice was offered or suggested at any point.

| 16 June 2023 | £3,199.00 | Invoice 876551 — Westcliff Lodge period |

### 1.4 Misrepresentation — Described as a Top-Up

The DPA was misrepresented by Jacquie Deacon as covering a **top-up** — the amount above the council's declared rate. The council's own ASC assessments of October 2022 and February 2023 record the financial discussion in terms of third party top-up contributions above the council's declared rate.

A DPA is not a top-up arrangement. It is a full cost loan secured against the property with no council funding contribution. The family signed an agreement for the full cost of care on the basis of a description of a fundamentally different arrangement. This constitutes misrepresentation under the Misrepresentation Act 1967 and a breach of the council's duty under Care and Support Statutory Guidance Chapter 9.

### 1.5 No Informed Consent

The Care and Support Statutory Guidance Chapter 9 requires the council to ensure the person and their representative fully understand the nature of a DPA before signing. There is no record of the council explaining that:

- The DPA is a full debt secured against the property
- Interest accrues on the full amount from day one
- The property is charged as security for the entire cost of care
- The debt becomes repayable within 90 days of the resident's death

### 1.6 No Independent Advice Offered

The Statutory Guidance requires councils to signpost people to independent financial advice before entering a DPA. No such signposting occurred at any stage.

### 1.7 No MCA Compliance

The council's own records confirm awareness of Bridget's lack of capacity from at least November 2020 (DoLS authorisation). The council held a four-year-old formal determination of Severe Mental Impairment — Class U Council Tax exemption granted 27 September 2019 — before the DPA was executed. No Best Interests decision preceded the DPA execution, no IMCA referral was made, and no meeting involving the Relevant Person's Representative occurred before signing.

## 3. Contractual Defects

### 3.1 No Frequency Stated for the £800 Charge

The DPA clause 1.1 defines Care Charges as **"£800.00"** with no stated frequency — no indication of whether this is per week, per four weeks, or per month. The Care and Support (Deferred Payments) Regulations 2014 require the DPA to clearly specify the care charges. A core financial term without a payment frequency does not meet this standard.

Under the *contra proferentem* rule, the ambiguity is construed against the council as drafter. The actual Westcliff Lodge fee was £738.46/week — meaning even on a weekly interpretation, the stated £800 figure exceeded the actual care home cost.

### 3.2 No Variation Ever Executed for Palmerston House

Clause 15.1 requires any variation to be **in writing and signed by both parties**. No written variation extending DPA coverage from Westcliff Lodge to Palmerston House has ever been executed or disclosed. The council has never produced any document showing the DPA was formally amended to reflect the change of accommodation.

### 3.3 DPA Has Never Covered Palmerston House

The Individual Placement Contract (IPC) dated 11 April 2023 — the council's own contract with Palmerston House — sets the weekly charge at **£750.00** and makes no reference to a DPA. This confirms the Palmerston placement was never formally brought within the scope of the existing DPA.

**Every care charge deferred since 11 April 2023 has accumulated against the property without any contractual authority under the DPA.**

The council's own complaint response describes Palmerston House as being "arranged under a Deferred Payment Scheme" — a statement that is factually incorrect as no DPA has ever covered that placement.

## 4. Financial Overcharging

### 4.1 IPC Proves £127.59/Week Overcharge at Palmerston

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| IPC rate — council's contract with Palmerston House | £750.00/week |
| Financial assessment total (contribution + deferred) | £877.59/week |
| **Weekly overcharge** | **£127.59/week** |

The Care and Support Statutory Guidance paragraph 8.14 prohibits councils from charging more than the cost they actually incur. The council's own IPC proves the actual cost was £750/week. The financial assessment applied from the same date calculated £877.59/week. Jacquie Deacon, named as Care Manager on the IPC, knew both figures simultaneously.

### 4.2 Double Billing at Westcliff Lodge

During the Westcliff Lodge period, the council simultaneously charged contributions of £800/week and deferred £389.77/week — a combined total of £1,189.77/week — against Westcliff Lodge's actual weekly fee of £738.46. The combined billing exceeded the actual care home fee by £451.31/week.

### 4.3 Wrong Attendance Allowance Rate Applied

The financial assessment applied **Attendance Allowance at the higher rate — £101.75/week**. The financial assessment form submitted by Mark Walker in October 2022 declared the lower rate of £240 per four weeks (approximately £60/week). This discrepancy of approximately £33.65/week has inflated Bridget's assessed contribution throughout the assessment period — an estimated cumulative overcharge of approximately £3,668 from April 2023 to present on the contribution element alone.

### 4.4 Capital Below Lower Threshold

The financial assessment form confirmed Bridget's total liquid capital as approximately **£8,692.94** — below the lower capital threshold of **£14,250**. With capital below the lower threshold, no tariff income applies and the council should have funded her care under Care Act s.18 from the outset, subject only to an income-based contribution. Had the mandatory property disregard also been applied, Bridget would have had no chargeable assets at all.

## 5. The Mandatory Property Disregard

### 5.1 The Disregard Should Have Applied

Under Schedule 2, paragraph 2(b) of the Care and Support (Charging and Assessment of Resources) Regulations 2014, the property must be disregarded from the financial assessment where a **dependent child of the person being assessed** occupies the property. Mark Walker's dependent child (born 4 April 2017) has lived at 84 Bellhouse Road throughout the charged period. This was declared on the DPA application form.

### 5.2 The Council's Legal Argument Is Wrong

The council's stated reason for refusing the disregard — *"you are not over 60 or a dependent child under 18"* — conflates two entirely separate legal provisions. The provision cited applies to a qualifying relative of the person being assessed. The dependent child provision (Schedule 2, para 2(b)) imposes no age requirement on the person claiming the disregard.

This legal error has been advanced in:
- Lynn Hyam's email of 13 November 2025
- The first formal complaint response (October 2025)
- The second formal complaint response (March 2026)

It was explicitly identified and corrected by Mark Walker in his November 2025 reply. It was not acknowledged or corrected in the March 2026 final response.

### 5.3 Consequence of Correct Application

Where the property disregard applies, **the property is taken out of the financial assessment entirely — meaning no DPA is needed and no charge can be placed.** Combined with Bridget's liquid capital being below the lower threshold, the DPA should never have been offered and the CH1 charge should never have been registered.

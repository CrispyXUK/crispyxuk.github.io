<h2 id="phase-1">Phase 1 — Sweyne Court (2020)</h2>

<h3>September 2020: Absconding Incident and Safeguarding Crisis</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>6 September 2020:</strong> Bridget absconds from Sweyne Court, located on Rayleigh High Street barefoot and unsupervised.</li>
  <li>CQC and safeguarding lead Jo Allen notified.</li>
  <li><strong>Immediate outcome:</strong> 24-hour one-to-one care imposed. Family directed to fund it personally. Invoice SI-32 from Champions Recruitment: £4,460.65.</li>
  <li><strong>Council position (Jo Allen):</strong> The Council stated it was "not legally obliged to support her financially" — a position that is unlawful under the Care Act 2014, which imposes a duty to meet eligible needs regardless of means.</li>
</ul>

<p>Two separate escape attempts are documented at Sweyne Court. Bridget presented a clear, evidenced wandering and elopement risk requiring continuous supervision — known to the Council from at least September 2020 onwards.</p>

<hr>
<h2 id="phase-2">Phase 2 — Admiral Court (September 2020 – September 2022)</h2>

<h3>Placement Context</h3>

<p>Bridget was admitted to Admiral Court (Hallmark) on approximately 16 September 2020 following the Sweyne Court absconding incident. Admiral Court is a <strong>registered nursing home</strong>, establishing an NHS-Funded Nursing Care obligation from the outset of this placement. The placement began as respite but became permanent due to Covid-19.</p>

<div style="background:#FCE4D6;border-left:4px solid #C00000;padding:10px 14px;border-radius:0 6px 6px 0;margin:1em 0;font-size:.9rem">
  <strong>Transfer care plan error (Issue <a href="{{ '/issues/' | relative_url }}#issue-12">#12</a>):</strong> The transfer care plan from Sweyne Court to Admiral Court (16 Sep 2020) recorded Bridget as "at low risk of leaving the home unnoticed" — written just 10 days after she had absconded barefoot to Rayleigh High Street. This incorrect risk recording fed into later care planning and funding assessments across the entire period.
</div>

<h3>Behavioural and Supervision Needs</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Behavioural issues documented upon admission to Admiral Court.</li>
  <li><strong>DoLS Standard Authorisation granted 10 November 2020</strong>, confirming: lack of mental capacity; continuous supervision required; Bridget is not free to leave; Kira Walker appointed as Relevant Person's Representative.</li>
  <li>Dementia Community Support Team visiting regularly from 2020 onwards.</li>
  <li>EPUT (Essex Partnership University NHS Trust) involvement documented throughout.</li>
  <li>All records on file with the Council.</li>
</ul>

<h3>No CHC Referral Despite Mandatory Triggers</h3>

<p>Capital was depleted from above £23,250 (July 2020) to below £14,250 by October 2022 through private nursing care fees of approximately £1,450 per week. Despite this, no CHC referral was initiated across the entire two-year placement, notwithstanding:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Active DoLS authorisation confirming complex needs and continuous supervision requirement — <a class="tl-ref" href="{{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}#tl-checklist-1">see timeline</a></li>
  <li>Registered nursing home placement triggering FNC obligations from day one <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 2">#2</span></li>
  <li>Continuous professional oversight from dementia services and EPUT</li>
  <li>Documented escape risks and supervision requirements following the September 2020 incident <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 1">#1</span> <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 3">#3</span></li>
</ul>

<hr>
<h2 id="phase-3">Phase 3 — Westcliff Lodge (September 2022 – April 2023)</h2>

<h3>Placement Rationale</h3>

<p>Bridget was transferred to Westcliff Lodge on 27–28 September 2022 following the Council's formal involvement under the Care Act 2014. The reason for the move was documented behavioural issues, escape risks, and the need for a specialist placement — the same risks that had been present since September 2020.</p>

<h3>Care Home Manager's Formal Warning <a class="tl-ref" href="{{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}#tl-westcliff-letter">15 Nov 2022</a></h3>

<div style="background:#FFF2CC;border-left:4px solid #C55A11;padding:10px 14px;border-radius:0 6px 6px 0;margin:1em 0;font-size:.9rem">
  <strong>Issue <a href="{{ '/issues/' | relative_url }}#issue-14">#14</a>:</strong> Westcliff Lodge manager Nicky Bourouisa wrote formally on 15 November 2022, warning of <strong>high-dependency behaviours requiring specialist placement</strong>. This warning was issued in the same month as CHC Checklist No. 1 (26 October 2022), which concluded the threshold for full DST was not met. The contemporaneous documentary evidence directly contradicts the checklist outcome.
</div>

<p>Documented risks at this stage included: wandering behaviour requiring one-to-one supervision; active escape attempts and elopement risk; conduct affecting the safety of other residents; and complex supervision needs explicitly flagged by a registered care professional.</p>

<hr>
<h2 id="phase-4">Phase 4 — Transfer to Palmerston House (April 2023)</h2>

<h3>Transfer Without Lawful Care Planning <a class="tl-ref" href="{{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}#tl-palmerston-transfer">11 Apr 2023</a></h3>

<div style="background:#FCE4D6;border-left:4px solid #C00000;padding:12px 16px;border-radius:0 6px 6px 0;margin:1em 0">
  <strong>Care Act s.25 failure (Issue <a href="{{ '/issues/' | relative_url }}#issue-30">#30</a>):</strong> No needs assessment was conducted at the point of transfer. No care plan was produced specifying supervision requirements. No risk assessment addressed wandering, elopement, or one-to-one care needs. No handover documentation communicated Bridget's behavioural history or supervision requirements to Palmerston staff.
</div>

<p>The Council placed Bridget at Palmerston <strong>knowing</strong> the placement at Westcliff Lodge had broken down due to her documented escape risks and behavioural complexity — yet produced no written care plan setting out the level of supervision required. This is a failure under Care Act 2014, section 25, which requires a written care and support plan specifying how eligible needs will be met.</p>

<h2 id="hospital">May 2023: Hospital Admission and Discharge Failure</h2>

<h3>Admission and Surgery</h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>15 May 2023:</strong> Unwitnessed fall at Palmerston House — fractured neck of femur.</li>
  <li>Emergency hospital admission and surgery.</li>
  <li>Duration: approximately 10 days. Discharge: on or around 25 May 2023.</li>
</ul>

<h3>In-Patient Safety Failure</h3>

<p>Post-operatively, Bridget fell out of bed due to absence of monitoring. The hospital did not have knowledge of:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Her documented escape and wandering risks</li>
  <li>Her continuous supervision requirements</li>
  <li>Her history of elopement attempts across three placements</li>
  <li>Her behavioural complexity and lack of capacity</li>
</ul>

<p>Only after the fall did the hospital move her to a monitored ward.</p>

<div style="background:#FFF2CC;border-left:4px solid #C55A11;padding:10px 14px;border-radius:0 6px 6px 0;margin:1em 0;font-size:.9rem">
  <strong>Root cause:</strong> The Council's failure to produce a care plan at the point of transfer to Palmerston meant that when Bridget was admitted to hospital, there was no documentation available to clinical staff setting out her supervision needs. The hospital had no way of knowing she required continuous monitoring. Family WhatsApp messages document the in-patient fall contemporaneously. No hospital incident report has been disclosed.
</div>

<h3>Discharge Documentation Failures <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 9">#9</span></h3>

<p>On discharge from hospital (c.25 May 2023):</p>

<h2 id="pattern">Systemic Pattern: Downplaying of Documented Needs</h2>

<h3>The Council's Knowledge at the Time of First CHC Checklist (26 October 2022)</h3>

<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Evidence on Council's record</th><th>Source</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Two escape attempts at Sweyne Court</td><td>Safeguarding records</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Absconding to Rayleigh High Street (barefoot)</td><td>CQC / Jo Allen notification</td></tr>
    <tr><td>DoLS authorisation — continuous supervision confirmed</td><td>10 Nov 2020 Standard Authorisation</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Two years of regular EPUT and DCST contact</td><td>Clinical records</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Westcliff Lodge manager's formal warning of high-dependency needs</td><td>Nicky Bourouisa letter, 15 Nov 2022</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Transfer care plan incorrectly recording "low risk of leaving home"</td><td>Sep 2020 transfer plan</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<p>Despite all of the above being on record, every CHC checklist produced between October 2022 and July 2025 screened Bridget <strong>out</strong> of full DST consideration. The Herbert Protocol entry of July 2025 records "no wandering risk" despite the entire documented history above <span class="issue-badge" title="Issue 13">#13</span>.</p>

<h3>The Financial Incentive</h3>

<p>By maintaining Bridget below the threshold for CHC referral, the Council avoided:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Transfer of full funding liability to NHS England under CHC</li>
  <li>NHS Funded Nursing Care payments during two years at a registered nursing home <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 2">#2</span></li>
  <li>The need to fund more intensive, appropriately supervised care under the Care Act <span class="issue-badge issue-badge-high" title="Issue 5">#5</span></li>
</ul>

<p>The pattern of evidence supports the family's position that Bridget's needs were systematically downplayed in order to avoid funding obligations — to the detriment of her safety, wellbeing, and health.</p>

<hr>
<h2 id="impact">Cumulative Impact on Bridget's Health and Wellbeing</h2>

<p>The cascade of failures documented above — no care plan at transfer, no supervision guidance to hospital, in-patient fall, no discharge documentation, no CHC assessment, no aftercare — contributed to Bridget's deterioration over the period 2022 to 2025.</p>

<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Indicator</th><th>Status (August 2025)</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr style="background:#FCE4D6"><td>Mobility</td><td>Bedbound</td></tr>
    <tr style="background:#FCE4D6"><td>Communication</td><td>Non-verbal</td></tr>
    <tr style="background:#FCE4D6"><td>Skin integrity (Waterlow)</td><td>27 — extreme pressure ulcer risk</td></tr>
    <tr style="background:#FCE4D6"><td>Continence</td><td>Doubly incontinent</td></tr>
    <tr style="background:#FCE4D6"><td>End of life</td><td>Active end-of-life care plan</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<p>These were not inevitable features of her illness. They were preventable failures in care planning, communication, and statutory compliance — failures that compounded over years and contributed materially to her deterioration and suffering.</p>

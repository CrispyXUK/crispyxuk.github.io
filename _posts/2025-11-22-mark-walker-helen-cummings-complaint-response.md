Thank you for your letter dated 30/10/2025 regarding the complaint relating to my mother, Bridget Walker. I acknowledge this has been confirmed as the final stage of the Council's internal complaints process.

However, your response does not address several key issues raised, and a number of points in the decision appear to be based on factual and procedural misunderstandings. For clarity of record and in line with audit principles under the Care Act 2014 and Local Government complaint standards, I am setting out the unresolved matters below.

---

### 1. Care Act Duties and Records Missing

Your letter refers predominantly to Continuing Healthcare decisions. My complaint did not challenge CHC eligibility — it challenged:

- the lack of any lawful Care Act Needs Assessment (s.9),
- no Eligibility Determination (s.13),
- no Care and Support Plan (s.25),
- no written Financial Assessment outcome (s.17),
- and no Best Interests record under the Mental Capacity Act.

None of these statutory documents have ever been supplied, and your letter does not confirm they exist.

Without these assessments, the charging decision and Deferred Payment Agreement were made **outside of the statutory framework**.

---

### 2. Mandatory Property Disregard Not Applied

The Council's written position is that the property was not disregarded because I am not "over 60 or a dependent child under 18."

That is not what the Care Act Statutory Guidance states.

The mandatory disregard applies **where a dependent child under 18 occupies the home**. It does not specify biological relationship or parental link.

My dependent child has lived at the address since 2019, and this was declared to the Council.

Therefore, the property should not have been included in the financial assessment, and the Deferred Payment Agreement should not have been issued.

Your response did not address this point.

---

### 3. Safeguarding and Capacity

You acknowledged the Council was aware my mother lacked capacity in 2020 due to the DoLS authorisation.

Despite this, no:

- Best Interests decision,
- IMCA referral,
- or meeting involving the Relevant Person's Representative

preceded the creation or signing of the Deferred Payment Agreement.

The Council therefore entered a financial and legal arrangement involving a person lacking capacity without applying MCA decision-making requirements.

Your response does not provide any lawful justification or evidence that this process was followed.

---

### 4. Backdating and Execution of the Deferred Payment Agreement

Charges and deferred amounts were applied before the agreement was signed. No fully executed copy of the agreement was returned to me, and the CH1 instrument submitted to HM Land Registry appears incomplete.

## Significance

**1. Council's first response addressed CHC only — six statutory failures unacknowledged**

The Council's complaint response of 30 October 2025 was routed through the Ageing Well team and addressed CHC eligibility only. Mark's reply identifies six separate statutory obligations that were never discharged and not mentioned in the response: s.9 needs assessment, s.13 eligibility determination, s.25 care and support plan, s.17 financial assessment, MCA Best Interests decision, and IMCA referral. The Council's failure to confirm the existence of any of these documents is itself evidentially significant. See Issues 30 and 31.

**2. Council's own property disregard position is legally wrong**

Helen Cummings's letter asserted the disregard did not apply because Mark was not "over 60 or a dependent child under 18." This misreads the Care Act Statutory Guidance (CSSG Annex B), which applies the mandatory disregard where *a* dependent child under 18 occupies the property — it is not limited to the care recipient's own biological child. The DPA Application Form (Section 3c, 16 Feb 2023) records Alex James Walker as a dependent child occupant. The Council's own form contradicts its own refusal. See Issues 21 and 22.

**3. DPA executed without MCA compliance — acknowledged by both parties**

Helen Cummings's covering email confirms the Council was aware Bridget lacked capacity since the 2020 DoLS authorisation. Mark's reply records that no Best Interests decision, IMCA referral, or RPR meeting preceded the DPA. The Council's response does not dispute this or provide any MCA documentation. This exchange is contemporaneous evidence that both parties understood Bridget lacked capacity and that no MCA process was applied. See Issue 26.

**4. Land Registry investigation confirmed in writing**

Mark states the Land Registry has confirmed the CH1 charge is under investigation. This is the first written record of that confirmation in the correspondence chain. The CH1 was registered on 12 June 2023 against a defective DPA that named the wrong care home and was signed (on its face) two days before it was scanned. See Issues 24, 25, 38, and 39.

**5. LGSCO referral and recovery suspension request**

The letter records that the LGSCO referral had been made and acknowledged at the time of writing (22 November 2025), preceding the formal acceptance date of 18 May 2026. The request to suspend all recovery activity pending the Ombudsman review was not granted — Pearl Adams subsequently chased payment in March–May 2026. See Issue 29.

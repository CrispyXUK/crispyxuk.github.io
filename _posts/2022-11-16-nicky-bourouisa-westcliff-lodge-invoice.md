## Significance

**1. Written in the same month as CHC Checklist #1 — direct contemporaneous contradiction**

CHC Checklist #1 was completed by Jacquie Deacon on 26 October 2022 — 20 days before this letter. That checklist recorded Behaviour as **C**: "No evidence of challenging behaviour." This letter, written by the registered manager of the care home where Bridget was living at the time, documents:
- Removing other residents' belongings (flowers, pictures, personal bedding)
- Destroying items
- Touching food that other residents were consuming
- Creating significant distress for other residents
- A formal recommendation that Bridget may require a higher-dependency placement

The checklist assessor and the care home manager were describing the same person at the same location in the same month. Their accounts are irreconcilable. Either the C score for Behaviour in Checklist #1 was wrong, or it was not based on any contact with the care home. See Issues 11 and 14.

**2. The care home's own assessment exceeds the CHC B threshold for Behaviour**

The CHC Behaviour domain B descriptor is: "Challenging behaviour that follows a predictable pattern. The risk assessment indicates a pattern of behaviour that can be managed by skilled carers." The B descriptor does not require specialist placement. The care home's letter goes further — Nicky Bourouisa states that Westcliff Lodge *cannot* manage Bridget's behaviours and that a higher-dependency specialist home is needed. This is consistent with the A (High) descriptor: "challenging behaviour of type and/or frequency that poses a predictable risk to self, others or property."

**3. The Behaviour score in Checklist #1 was C — nine months later it became B in Checklist #3**

The scoring progression across the checklists for Behaviour is: C (Oct 2022) → B (Feb 2023) → B (Sep 2023) → B (May 2024) → C (Jun 2025). The upgrade from C to B between Checklists #1 and #2 may itself reflect pressure from family or care home feedback. But the contemporaneous written evidence from the care home manager — produced at the time of Checklist #1 — demonstrates that C was wrong from the outset.

**4. Bridget was asked to leave Westcliff Lodge**

This letter foreshadows Bridget's eventual move. She was asked to leave Westcliff Lodge and transferred to Palmerston House on 11 April 2023 — approximately five months after this letter. The stated reason was consistent with the behaviours described here. No CHC assessment was conducted at the point of the move, and no needs assessment or care plan was prepared for the Palmerston placement. See Issues 30 and 31.

**5. Care home manager's contact through NHS mail**

Nicky Bourouisa's forwarded email comes from her NHSmail address (nicola.bourouisa@nhs.net), confirming she was an NHS-registered care professional. The letter was sent in her capacity as Registered Manager of a CQC-regulated care home. It is a formal professional communication, not an informal family note, and carries evidential weight accordingly.

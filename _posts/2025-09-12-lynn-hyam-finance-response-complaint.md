## Significance

**1. Mark's email copied to CQC and NHS ICB — formal complaint escalation on 3 September 2025**

Mark's email of 3 September 2025 was sent simultaneously to Social Care Income, CQC (ref CAS-1035468-Y7J0G4), Lynn Hyam, and the NHS ICB complaints address. This is the day the formal complaint was submitted across all three bodies. The email itself constitutes the contemporaneous complaint record and establishes the specific issues raised at the point of submission.

**2. Mark identifies the day count error in real time**

Mark's calculation shows the Council's invoice for the DPA commencement period (20 Dec 2022 – 5 Mar 2023) used 76 days instead of 75 — a one-day overcharge. The Council charged £114.29 × 76 = £8,685.71 when the correct figure was £114.29 × 75 = £8,571.43. This £114.29 arithmetic error is separately documented in real time in this email chain.

**3. Council acknowledges the backdating of the DPA start date**

The Council's earlier response (quoted by Mark) states: "If the start date had not been backdated you would have been invoiced the full cost of the Care Home." This is an explicit Council admission that the DPA start date was backdated. Mark's response — "so you agree with me then?" — goes unanswered. The admission that the start date was backdated is significant because it is inconsistent with the DPA being a contemporaneous document executed on 19 December 2022. See Issues 24 and 39.

**4. Council acknowledges no updated DPA was provided**

Mark states: "We've still not received an updated DPA or contract" after the Palmerston move. The Council does not dispute this. Lynn Hyam's response makes no reference to the absence of a DPA variation for Palmerston. The failure to issue a written variation in breach of DPA clause 15.1 is thereby implicitly confirmed. See Issue 25.

**5. Finance Team explicitly disclaim knowledge of care and social care matters**

Lynn Hyam's response states: "For queries regarding care and support plans, annual reviews, case notes, funding eligibility decisions, NHS Funded Nursing Care or DoLS, these matters need to be discussed with the allocated Social Worker. I regret the Finance Team do not have access or the knowledge to respond to these matters." This disclaimer — made in writing — confirms that the Finance Team was handling the DPA and invoicing in isolation from the care planning function. The MCA compliance for the DPA, which is a social care function, was therefore never within the Finance Team's remit. This supports the argument that the DPA was executed without the MCA oversight that social care should have provided. See Issue 26.

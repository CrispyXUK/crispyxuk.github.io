## Significance

**1. Safeguarding lead Jo Allen was notified — and took no action**

Hayley Gibbs confirms she spoke to Jo Allen, safeguarding lead at Southend-on-Sea Borough Council's social services. Jo Allen was aware that Bridget had absconded, was posing a risk to herself and others, and lacked awareness of danger. The Council's response, as relayed by the care home, was that because Bridget was a private payer they were not obliged to support her financially — and that they were satisfied the 1:1 care arrangement was sufficient.

This is the Council's safeguarding lead acknowledging a safeguarding situation and choosing not to intervene substantively. The Care Act 2014 safeguarding duties (s.42) are not disapplied because a person is self-funding. The Council had a duty to make enquiries where a person with care needs appeared to be at risk of abuse or neglect, regardless of funding status.

**2. Transfer care plan error — established here**

This email confirms the absconding incident occurred before 10 September 2020. Bridget left the building unaccompanied, posing a risk to herself and others. She had no awareness of danger. Yet the transfer care plan to Admiral Court — dated approximately 16 September 2020, just six days after this email — recorded Bridget as "at low risk of leaving the home unnoticed." This directly contradicts the care home's own account in this email. See Issue 12.

**3. Financial liability imposed on family unlawfully**

The care home told Kira: "This will be your responsibility to pay as you hold LPA and Mum pays privately — you are legally obliged to provide that care for her." This is legally incorrect. Holding LPA does not make an attorney personally liable for a care recipient's care costs. The attorney acts on behalf of the person — they are not personally obligated to fund care from their own resources. The family went on to pay £4,460.65 (Champions Recruitment Invoice SI-32) under this misapprehension, communicated in writing by the care home and implicitly endorsed by the Council's safeguarding lead.

**4. Council involvement confirmed from 10 September 2020**

This email is dated 10 September 2020 — predating the formal self-assessment (27 July 2020 service request) being passed to the Access Team. Combined with the April 2020 email from Kira to the Dementia Support Team, this establishes that the Council had actual knowledge of Bridget's complex and deteriorating needs from at least April 2020, with a specific safeguarding notification on 10 September 2020. The Council's later treatment of its involvement as beginning only with the Care Act assessment in November 2022 is inconsistent with this documented history.

**5. No FNC consideration despite nursing-level needs**

Bridget was assessed as requiring 24-hour 1:1 care due to risk of absconding, self-harm, and harm to others. She had no awareness of danger. These are clinical-level needs. At no point in this exchange — or in any subsequent record — did the Council or the Dementia Crisis Team consider whether Bridget might be eligible for NHS Continuing Healthcare or NHS-Funded Nursing Care. She was subsequently admitted to Admiral Court, a registered nursing home, where no FNC assessment was ever conducted. See Issues 1 and 2.

## Significance

**1. Mark's clearest statement of the central Care Act case — on record before the second response**

This email, written on 17 February 2026, is the most direct and complete statement of the Care Act failure argument produced before the Council's second final response (11 March 2026). It identifies three specific statutory obligations — s.9 needs assessment, s.13 eligibility determination, s.25 care and support plan — none of which were ever provided. The second complaint response was issued 22 days after this email, having been prompted by it. It failed to address any of these three points substantively. See Issues 30 and 31.

**2. "My principal concern is not billing in isolation"**

This framing is important for the LGSCO. The Council's complaint handling consistently treated the complaint as primarily a financial dispute. Mark's email on the record — before the second response — makes clear that the financial failures are secondary to the systemic Care Act failure. The absence of a lawful assessment framework is the root cause from which the financial failures flow: no needs assessment meant no proper care planning; no proper care planning meant Bridget was at Palmerston House without a valid care plan; without a valid care plan and DPA, the financial basis for all charges is undermined.

**3. NHS and EPUT hold no records prior to 2025**

Mark formally records in this email that both the NHS and EPUT confirmed they hold no records relating to Bridget prior to 2025, despite documented Dementia Crisis Team involvement in 2020. This is a significant gap in the inter-agency record — it means the safeguarding notification made to Jo Allen in September 2020, and the Dementia Crisis Team's involvement, left no traceable record in the NHS or EPUT systems. See Issue 47.

**4. The Council's own safeguarding lead was named — and the private-funding argument rebutted**

Mark names Jo Allen, the Council's own safeguarding lead, as having been informed of the September 2020 situation. He then notes the advice that social services were "not obliged to provide support" because Bridget was privately funded — and frames this as the reason the family funded care privately. This is the contemporaneous record of the Council's own position at the time of the safeguarding incident, which is directly relevant to the CHC referral failures and the financial depletion of capital. See Issues 1 and 10.

**5. The email prompted the second disclosure bundle**

This email was sent on 17 February 2026. The Council's second final response was issued on 11 March 2026 — 22 days later — accompanied by the first disclosure of all CHC checklists and ASC assessments. The disclosure was a direct response to this email. The bundle was provided at the point the complaint was being closed, not in advance of the response to allow Mark to engage with the documents before the complaint was exhausted.

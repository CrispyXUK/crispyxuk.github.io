## Significance

**1. The visit was described as a "care review" — not a DoLS BIA**

Littman's original email to Kira (29 May 2026) described the visit as a "care review" with no mention of a DoLS Best Interests Assessment. Mark learned the true nature of the visit from Daisy Naidoo, the Deputy Manager of Palmerston House. When challenged, Littman clarifies he is "not a DoLS Best Interests Assessor" and is "undertaking a care review." This does not resolve the question of who is conducting the DoLS BIA — Littman says he has forwarded the request to the "relevant DoLS team." The DoLS BIA remains undisclosed and unconfirmed. See Issues 33 and 44.

**2. Mark notified only via Kira forwarding an email — not directly**

Mark's email records explicitly: "I became aware of it only when my sister Kira forwarded an email she received from you on Friday 29 May 2026." This is a live, current instance of the selective LPA communication pattern — health/welfare contact directed to Kira only, Mark excluded from direct notification despite holding identical joint LPA authority. It occurs during the active LGSCO investigation and is contemporaneous evidence of the pattern documented across issues 43 and 44.

**3. Littman confirms LPA held jointly — but had not been contacting both**

Littman acknowledges "our records currently show that you hold joint Lasting Power of Attorney" and asks for a copy of the LPA documentation. The LPA has been held jointly since its execution. The Council's own records show this. Despite this, Littman's initial contact was directed to Kira alone. His request for LPA documentation — when it is already on the Council's system and has been in place throughout — suggests either a records failure or an attempt to reframe the situation as an oversight.

**4. "Since 2013 the family has never been formally invited to attend any care process"**

Mark's email contains the key statement: "since my mother's diagnosis with Alzheimer's disease in 2013, the family has never been formally invited to attend a care review, assessment or planning meeting by the Council. The only exception was the CHC Decision Support Tool meeting in August 2025." This is a 13-year period during which the family was excluded from formal care processes — with a single exception in August 2025, and even that was the NHS rather than the Council. This statement, on the record, is directly relevant to the LGSCO complaint.

**5. "GDPR regulations" cited to remove an email from the trail**

Littman states: "Due to our records not having the other email cc'd in I have had to remove from the email trail due to GDPR regulations." This is not a valid application of GDPR. GDPR does not prevent copying a correspondence chain to both attorneys. The effect of removing the email from the trail is to reduce the written record of what was communicated to Mark — which is consistent with the documented pattern of health/welfare communications being handled outside written channels. See Issue 43.

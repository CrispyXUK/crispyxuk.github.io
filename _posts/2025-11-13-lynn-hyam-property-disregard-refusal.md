## Significance

**1. The Council refuses the disregard on the basis the family moved in after Bridget entered care**

Lynn Hyam's position is that the mandatory property disregard does not apply because the family moved into 84 Bellhouse Road on 2 March 2021, after Bridget entered residential care in 2020. The disregard under Schedule 2 of the Care and Support (Charging and Assessment of Resources) Regulations 2014 requires continuous occupation since before the person entered care. The Council's case is that this condition is not met.

**2. The DPA application form directly contradicts this position**

The DPA Application Form signed by Mark on 16 February 2023 — on the Council's own form — declares in Section 3c: "Does anyone live in the property — Yes" and lists:
- Mark James Walker — DOB 20/10/1980
- Victoria Elizabeth Walker — DOB 16/2/1983
- Alex James Walker (dependent child, no DOB given)

The Council issued a DPA, registered a legal charge against 84 Bellhouse Road, and accepted a signed declaration that the family were occupying the property — yet simultaneously refused the disregard that should have applied because of that occupation. The Council cannot simultaneously rely on the occupation for the DPA charge and deny it for the disregard. See Issue 21.

**3. The Council's stated move-in date of 2 March 2021 is disputed**

Mark's reply ("Not, section 34. I can also include myself.") and the DPA application form both indicate the family's position on occupancy. The Council's figure of 2 March 2021 comes from its own records — it has never disclosed the basis for this date or the documents on which it relies. The family's actual move-in date, evidenced by tenancy records, utilities, and the DPA application itself, needs to be verified and formally put to the Council and the LGSCO. See Issue 22.

**4. "You should have the funds to settle these charges from her pension"**

Lynn Hyam's instruction that Mark should settle the arrears of £24,265.63 from Bridget's pension and Attendance Allowance is notable. An attorney acting under LPA for a person lacking capacity is not personally liable for the care recipient's debts. The Council was directing an attorney — as if he were personally responsible — to pay disputed charges from income belonging to the person under care. Attendance Allowance is a benefit paid to meet care needs; the suggestion that it should be applied to arrears on a disputed and unreconciled account conflates its purpose.

**5. £24,265.63 demanded — during active complaint handling**

This demand was issued on 13 November 2025, while the formal complaint was being processed (submitted 5 September 2025, first response 30 October 2025). The Council was pursuing aggressive debt recovery correspondence — including a specific figure and a demand to pay — less than two weeks after issuing its first complaint response. See Issue 29.

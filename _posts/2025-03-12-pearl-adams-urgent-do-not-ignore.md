## Significance

**1. Recovery action being pursued against a disputed and unreconciled account**

By January 2025, Mark had already identified significant discrepancies in the Council's invoicing — the £127.59/week overcharge against Palmerston's actual contractual rate was documented in his August 2025 demand letter (which referenced earlier calculations). The Council was pursuing recovery of an account that had not been properly reconciled and where the underlying charges were disputed. The use of "URGENT DO NOT IGNORE" as a subject line for a formal debt chase against an LPA attorney for a vulnerable adult's disputed care fees is an aggressive and disproportionate approach. See Issue 29.

**2. No payments made since September 2024 — the family's position**

The emails confirm no payment had been made since September 2024. This aligns with the period in which Mark had begun formally questioning the financial basis of the charges. The outstanding balance at this point was growing — the August 2025 Lynn Hyam email states £20,705.39 unpaid from monthly invoices since September 2024, with the DPA charge in addition. Mark's position was that the figures were wrong and needed reconciliation before any payment could be considered.

**3. Threatening language — "further action"**

The March 2025 email warns that "further action" will be taken if Mark does not contact the Council. This is debt collection language directed at someone acting as an attorney for a person who lacked capacity, in respect of charges that were disputed on multiple grounds, at a time when the formal complaint had not yet been submitted. No reconciliation or breakdown had been provided in response to Mark's earlier requests.

**4. Contrast with the Council's own delay**

Pearl Adams chased in January and March 2025 for payments overdue since September 2024 — a delay of 4–6 months. The Council's first complaint response took 56 days from submission (5 September to 30 October 2025). The Council applied urgency standards to debt recovery that it did not apply to its own complaint handling obligations.

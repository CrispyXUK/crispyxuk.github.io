## Significance

**1. FNC denied in writing on a specific stated basis**

Lynne Gardiner's email of 2 September 2025 contains the sentence: "I understand that Mercy explained that due to Mum living in a residential home and not a nursing home she is not entitled to FNC unfortunately." This is a written Council record of a specific legal position being communicated to the family: FNC not available because Palmerston House is a residential home. The CQC registration for Palmerston House (location 1-156343233) confirms it is registered as a residential home with an express condition prohibiting nursing care — so the factual basis of the denial is technically accurate. However, this immediately raises the placement appropriateness question: if Palmerston House cannot provide nursing care, and Bridget has nursing-level needs (2-staff hoist, covert medication, DNACPR, Waterlow 27), why is she placed there? Neither Gardiner nor anyone else has addressed this. See Issues 4 and 5.

**2. The "Mercy" meeting is an undisclosed CHC consideration meeting**

Gardiner refers to a colleague named Mercy who attended "a meeting to complete consideration for continuing health funding assessment with an NHS colleague" while Gardiner was off work. No record, agenda, minutes, invitation or outcome of this meeting has ever been disclosed to the family. It preceded the formal DST meeting of 29 August 2025 and appears to be a separate CHC consideration or checklist exercise. The family were not invited, not notified, and have never received any documentation from it. See Issue 6.

**3. Medical conditions added to the system — retrospectively**

Gardiner confirms she will "ensure this is also documented on our system" in relation to the cancer diagnosis, prolapse, and skin integrity issues Mark raised. This means these conditions were not already on the Council's system — they were added in September 2025, after Mark raised them, years after they occurred. The cancer diagnosis was December 2023. The prolapse was documented in care home records from 2023. The skin integrity issues date from the April 2023 hip fracture. These conditions were not reflected in the May 2024 CHC Checklist, and the Council's system apparently did not record them at all until September 2025. See Issue 10.

**4. Sweyne Court safeguarding deflected to Essex County Council**

Gardiner deflects the September 2020 safeguarding incident to Essex County Council on the basis that Sweyne Court was in the Essex area. While jurisdictionally accurate, this does not address the broader point: Southend's own safeguarding lead (Jo Allen) was personally informed of the situation on 10 September 2020 and took no action to initiate an assessment. The response that "Southend City Council would not have any record" of the safeguarding is therefore incomplete — Jo Allen's knowledge is not addressed. See Issue 10.

**5. This email is sent to both Mark and Kira — an exception to the pattern**

Gardiner sends this email to both Mark (crispybaconx@icloud.com) and Kira (kira_oz@hotmail.com). This is notable because the consistent pattern throughout the case has been for health/welfare communications to be directed to Kira and financial communications to Mark. Mark's email to Gardiner explicitly copied Kira. Gardiner's reply to both is responsive but does not reflect a systemic change in the Council's approach — subsequent communications continued the selective pattern. See Issue 43.
